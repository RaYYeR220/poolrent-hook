# Evidence

Local evidence record for one exact submission revision of `poolrent`. Everything here is
**builder-declared and untrusted** until a maintainer reproduces it. Local tool results are kept
separate from independent review, deployment receipts, source verification, runtime matching,
lifecycle proof, routing review and product availability — none of which exist for this revision.

All four executed gross quote-side cases are covered, in both the `beforeSwap` and the `afterSwap`
quadrants, with the executed amount — not the requested amount — as the basis. Rounding is
directional and declared: down on the legs carved out of a known gross amount, up on the legs
grossed onto a net amount, and up for the platform's half of each split so per-swap rounding can
never leave the immutable owner short. Owner-only claims are proven against the immutable address,
including owner-selected per-claim destinations, and against failed attempts by the builder, the
project, an administrator-like caller, the launcher, a manager and arbitrary addresses. There is no
stored mutable recipient, rescue, sweep or redirect path; storage-overwrite attempts leave the
constant owner unchanged. Accrual plus partial and full claims reconcile to the remaining liability
and the backing balance to the wei.

The quote-asset-derived before/after return-delta path is asserted per swap mode. The self-call
policy is `same-pool-swap-fee-enforced-internally`, which the standard requires from any hook that
makes direct PoolManager calls. This hook makes exactly four — `donate`, `take`, `sync` and `settle`,
all inside `afterSwap` — and never `swap` or `modifyLiquidity`, so there is no hook-initiated
same-pool swap for v4's self-call callback suppression to skip a charge on. A test proves no path
from the hook reaches `PoolManager.swap`.

## Fee-conformance correction, revision 2

The first revision floored the combined 20 bps charge independently on every swap and carried no
cumulative remainder, so an entitlement worth less than a wei was destroyed rather than deferred: a
thousand 499-wei swaps paid the immutable owner nothing, while the identical 499,000 wei of aggregate
volume owes it 499 wei. Splitting the already-floored total could not recover it.

This revision accrues each entitlement directly from the executed gross against its own persisted
numerator remainder — `platformFeeCarry` and `projectFeeCarry`, both public — at independent rates of
1000 and 1000 hundredths of a basis point. Claiming moves `feeOwed` and never touches a remainder.
The project remainder is conserved across handovers, evictions and the vacant-seat path. The
exact-output legs now solve for the gross that leaves the trader their exact net, bounded and failing
closed. A unit withheld by the "a charge may never consume the whole executed amount" bound is
returned to the remainder, project side first, rather than dropped.

The maintainer's scenario is pinned verbatim as a regression: 1,000 swaps of 499 wei gross now pay
the platform exactly 499 wei, with `feeFromGross(499, PLATFORM_RATE) == 0` asserted first so the test
fails loudly if per-swap flooring ever returns. Conservation is asserted as an equality — not a dust
band — at three levels: the library over random sequences, the hook over random swap runs, and the
stateful invariant suite over 16,384 generated calls per invariant.

## Fee-quantum conformance, revision 3

The current fee kernel requires that a nonzero executed gross below 1,000 smallest quote units reverts
atomically, so that every accepted swap can fund each whole unit its carried remainders realise. The
hook enforces it with `MIN_GROSS_QUOTE_UNITS` and `GrossBelowFeeQuantum`, on the executed gross, in
`beforeSwap` for the two quote-specified quadrants and in `afterSwap` for the other two. On an
18-decimal quote asset the threshold is 1e-15 of a token.

The quantum does not replace the carried remainders — the two are complementary, and both are
required. The quantum guarantees an accepted swap funds a whole unit; the carry conserves the fraction
above it, so splitting accepted volume still cannot suppress the entitlement. The regression that
pinned the original review finding was moved one step above the quantum and keeps the same numbers:
1,000 swaps of 1,499 units pay 1,499 where per-swap flooring pays 1,000, and the 499-unit difference
the carry recovers is the quantity the review was about.

**One consequence stated rather than left to be discovered.** With the quantum in force, the clamp
that prevents a charge from consuming the whole executed amount can no longer bind on-chain: for any
accepted gross the combined charge is far smaller. Its write-back branch in `PoolRentHook` is
therefore unreachable in production. It is retained as defensive depth, its arithmetic is still
covered at the library level, and its unreachability is proven by a 1,000-run fuzz property rather
than asserted in prose. A reviewer should read that branch as deliberately redundant, not untested.

## Reproducing the deployed PoolManager

This is the answer to "is any of this real?". The Uniswap v4 `PoolManager` the hook is bound to was
rebuilt from the source pinned in this repository and compared with the code running on Ethereum
mainnet. Full record in `evidence/deployment-v4-poolmanager-ethereum.json`.

| Step | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Source | `lib/v4-core` at `59d3ecf53afa9264a16bba0e38f4c5d2231f80bc`, tree `eb2b71f69fae17a743bcdddd54b5fe941236d949`, as checked into this repository |
| Compiler | the official `solc 0.8.26+commit.8a97fa7a` binary, run over a standard-JSON input of 45 sources |
| Settings | Uniswap's own, from `lib/v4-core/foundry.toml` at that revision: `viaIR` on, optimizer at 44,444,444 runs, EVM Cancun, `bytecode_hash` none. Independently corroborated by the deployed runtime's own CBOR trailer, `a1 64 736f6c63 43 00 08 1a` — solc 0.8.26, no metadata hash |
| Observed runtime | 24,009 bytes at `0x000000000004444c5dc75cB358380D2e3dE08A90`, read from an archive RPC at block 25,700,000 and confirmed unchanged at 25,701,684 / 25,701,690 / 25,701,767 |

| Explanation | that slot is the Solidity immutable `NoDelegateCall.original`. The compiler emits a zero placeholder; the deployment substitutes the contract's own address. The surrounding opcodes are `AND ADDRESS EQ PUSH2 JUMPI`, the delegatecall guard |

Two consequences. The `revision` recorded for the PoolManager dependency is proven rather than
cited — and note that it is *not* the `v4-core@1.0.0` tag the pinned deployment feed names, which the
feed itself marks `sourceRefResolution: unresolved`; `git describe` calls this commit
`v4.0.0-12-g59d3ecf5`. And the dependency's `sourceStatus` is `matched` because a reproduction was
actually performed here, not because an explorer says so. No explorer or third-party verification
service was consulted. A source-to-runtime reproduction is still not a deployment receipt and not an
audit: it proves the bytes running at that address are the bytes this repository compiles.

## Verifier findings, resolved

`verify-package` reports no errors on this revision. Three earlier findings were closed by doing the
work rather than by relaxing a claim, and the way each was closed is worth stating:

1. **The PoolManager `revision` and `sourceStatus`.** Previously the revision was left null and the
   status was `runtime-observed-source-not-reproduced`, because nobody had rebuilt the bytecode.
   Somebody has now: the reproduction above is exact, so the revision is proven and `matched` is a
   true statement.
2. **`solmate/src/auth/Owned.sol` was not covered by the dependency lock.** It is reached only
   through Uniswap v4 Core's own vendored checkout at `lib/v4-core/lib/solmate`, because v4 Core's
   `ProtocolFees` imports `Owned` and the suite deploys a real `PoolManager`; no contract under
   `src/` imports it. The `programmable-tested` set is frozen and cannot carry a transitive entry, so
   the lock is recorded as `model-specific-pinned`: the same baseline pins, byte for byte, plus that
   one solmate revision. The cost is the two extra candidate gates noted above. Any submission that
   instantiates a real PoolManager in its tests will meet the same wall.
3. **WETH9 resolved to no committed deployment record.** It never could — it is in none of the
   references the standard ships. It is now classified as what it is, the pool's quote asset and an
   external-call target, with its address, observed runtime hash and evidence file all retained.

Three warnings remain and are expected to stay: `test/Adversarial.t.sol`, `test/Auction.t.sol` and
`test/Fee.t.sol` each contain a low-level `.call`. Those are the negative-authorization probes — they
call selectors that deliberately do not exist and assert the call fails — so they cannot be written
as typed calls. The disposition is recorded in `TEST_PLAN.md`; the verifier's check is an
unconditional source regex and no artifact can clear it.

## What this evidence does not prove

It does not prove that the contracts are audited, accepted, integrated, deployed, verified, routed,
supported by any provider, or available — and it does not prove that the mandatory fee is collected
live anywhere. It proves that at one exact commit, under one exact toolchain, the described
behaviour is what the code does, including against the contracts currently deployed on Ethereum
mainnet.
